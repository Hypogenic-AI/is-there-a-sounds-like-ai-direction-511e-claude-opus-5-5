# Notes: Steer-to-Detect (S2D) — Liang & Li, NeurIPS 2026 (arXiv 2605.12890v2)

Deep read: all 46 pages (16 chunks), including appendices and proofs.
Code: https://github.com/LuxLiang/steer-to-detect-release/tree/main

## TL;DR
S2D is a **detector**, not a generation-steering method. A single learnable vector `v` is
**added to the residual stream of a frozen "observer" LLM (Llama-3.1-8B by default) while it reads
the input text**. `v` is trained by gradient descent so that the observer's pooled late-layer
representation separates human vs LLM text better. The detection score is the projection of the
steered, normalized representation onto `mu_1 - mu_0`, thresholded. **They never steer the
model's own generation and never ask whether `v` changes what the model writes.** So the paper gives
evidence for (a) separability, and tells us nothing about (b) generation-time causality or
(c) whether the direction is distinct from confounds.

## Method
- **Observer models**: default `meta-llama/Llama-3.1-8B` (base). Also Llama-3.2-1B, Meta-Llama-3-8B,
  Meta-Llama-3-70B, Mistral-7B-v0.3, GPT-Neo-2.7B, OPT-2.7B, Qwen2.5-7B, Falcon-7B(-Instruct),
  Gemma-2-9B(-Instruct). The observer stays frozen. Only `v` (d-dim) is trained.
- **Representation f(x)**: average the hidden states over the **last N=8 layers** and the
  **last K=25% of non-padding tokens**, then L2-normalize onto the unit sphere. The authors argue
  that after RMSNorm the norms are nearly constant (Llama-3.1-8B mean pooled norm ~18.8, sd ~1.1),
  so the information is in the direction. Each class looks unimodal on the sphere, which motivates
  a von Mises-Fisher (vMF) model.
- **Intervention (yes, a real activation intervention)**: `h_{l,t} <- h_{l,t} + v` at **one
  intermediate layer (default layer 11 of 32, applied to the residual connection)**, at **every
  valid token position**. The steered activations then pass through the remaining frozen blocks.
  It is an additive intervention with a single fixed vector, the same for every input, and it runs
  at read time (forward pass on given text), not at generation time.
- **Training `v` (Phase I)**: maximize the softmax/vMF posterior
  `q(y|f) = softmax(beta * mu_y^T f_v(x))` with beta=2.5. Uses a two-timescale scheme:
  `v` is updated with AdamW (lr 1e-3, batch 8, 10 epochs), and the class means `mu_c` are tracked
  by EMA (decay 0.9) of the normalized class-batch means. `v` is initialized to 0.
- **Detection (Phase II)**: score `S(x) = (mu_1 - mu_0)^T f_v(x)`, a likelihood-ratio test under a
  shared-kappa vMF model. The threshold is calibrated on a human-only null set to give a target
  FPR alpha. They also report a Youden-index threshold.
- **Theory**: finite-sample Type-I control `|P0(reject) - alpha| <= sqrt(log(2/delta)/(2 n2)) + 1/n2`
  (DKW). They bound the excess Type-II error (EMA tracking error plus calibration error) under a
  local vMF assumption, and give a Wasserstein-shift bound. The theory does not cover global
  convergence of `v`.

## Datasets
- **DetectRL** (Wu et al. 2024) with four domains: arXiv abstracts, XSum news, WritingPrompts
  stories and Yelp reviews. There are 2,800 human/LLM pairs per domain. Generators are
  GPT-3.5-Turbo, Claude-Instant, Google-PaLM and Llama-2-70B. Each run samples 512 training pairs
  and uses a fixed, disjoint test set of 1,000 pairs. Results are over 5 runs. Mean test length is
  ~267 tokens (95% of texts are under 435).
- **OpAI-Bench** (2026): progressive human-to-AI revision trajectories (v4/v6/v8, roughly
  50/75/100% of sentences edited). Generators are GPT-5.4, GPT-5.4-nano, Gemini-2.5-Flash and
  Qwen3-8B. Domains are abstracts, essays, news and reports. Uses 512 training pairs and 302 test
  documents.
- Large null set for low-FPR validation: 21,686 human and 20,000 LLM texts from C-ReD, EvoBench and
  OpAI-Bench. A long-context set (256 to 4096 tokens) is built from the same sources.
- Watermark comparison: Qwen2.5-7B generations with Gumbel-max, Green-red (KGW) and SynthID.

## Baselines
- **Training-free**: Likelihood, LRR, Fast-DetectGPT, Binoculars, IRM, Lastde++, SpecDetect++, NTS.
- **Train-based**: RoBERTa-Large, RAIDAR, ImBD, RepreGuard (a hidden-representation detector and
  the closest prior work), L2D (Learn-to-Distance, rewrite-based).
- **Probes on unsteered pooled representations**: logistic regression, linear SVM, small MLP. These
  are important for us.
- Default watermark detectors.
- All methods share Llama-3.1-8B as the backbone. Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct is used as the rewriter and
  as the Binoculars performer.

## Metrics
AUROC, TPR@1% FPR and TPR@0.01% FPR. TPR@0.01% is interpolated, because 1,000 human test texts give
a resolution of only 0.1%; Table 7 validates the interpolation on the large null set. They also
report the score-distribution "overlap" between classes.

## Key numbers
- **ID/OOD (Table 2)**: in-distribution AUROC is about 99.9-100. The hardest OOD targets are PaLM
  and Claude. For example, trained on ChatGPT and tested on Claude: S2D AUROC 98.31, TPR@0.01%
  79.48, compared with RepreGuard 98.25/70.40 and L2D 83.80/12.30. Trained on Claude and tested on
  PaLM: S2D 98.92/89.45.
- **Steering vs no steering (Fig. 4, Fig. 7)**: score overlap falls from 0.144 to 0.043 on the
  mixed set. On OOD Claude it falls from 14.1% to 4.4%, and on PaLM from 18.3% to 4.0%. Raw
  unsteered late-layer representations already separate ID data well (overlap ~0.8%).
- **Linear probes on unsteered representations (Table 12, OpAI v4/v6/v8)**: the probes come close
  to S2D. At v8, LogReg gets ID 0.99/0.89 and OOD 0.96/0.79 (AUROC/TPR@1%), while S2D gets
  0.99/0.92 and 0.97/0.81. **Most of the separability is already linearly available in the
  unsteered residual stream.** The authors admit this comparison does not separate the
  contribution of steering from that of the readout.
- **Robustness (Table 3, mixed)**: no attack 98.87/96.68. Under Polish paraphrase, 82.06/23.60
  (TPR drops 76%). Under back-translation, 93.45/67.33. Under DIPPER, 98.66/84.91. Character and
  word perturbations cost only a little. **LLM polishing/paraphrasing is the main weakness for every
  detector.**
- **Steering layer (Fig. 2b, Table 10)**: very early injection is poor. Intermediate depth
  (r = l/L ≈ 0.25-0.6) works well. Whether the vector is injected in the residual stream, the
  attention output or the MLP output matters little. Best layers: Llama-3.2-1B layer 4/16,
  Llama-3-8B layer 12/32, Llama-3-70B layer 40/80. There is no clean proportional depth rule.
- **Pooling (Fig. 2c)**: the effect is non-monotonic. Two configurations work well: (few top
  layers, a small fraction of the last tokens) and (many layers, a large fraction of tokens).
- **Observers (Table 9, average AUROC/TPR@0.01%)**: Llama-3.1-8B 99.62/97.98, Qwen2.5-7B
  99.93/98.65, Falcon-7B 99.96/98.28, OPT-2.7B 99.86/84.50. Weak observers: Mistral-7B 68.5/23.3,
  GPT-Neo-2.7B 82.4/11.3, Gemma-2-9B 76.5/3.1. Instruction tuning of the observer does not
  consistently help.
- **Few-shot**: performance is strong even with 16 training pairs.
- **Length**: AUROC 0.91 at 256 tokens, 0.99 at 1024 and about 1.0 at 2048+. Repeatedly injecting
  `v` does not cause collapse.
- **Watermarks**: at T=0.3 and 256 tokens S2D reaches about 99.9 AUROC (it picks up low-entropy
  artifacts). At T=0.7 and short lengths it is worse than the watermark detectors.
- **Cost**: inference takes 0.30 s per sample, compared with 2.03 s for L2D (excluding rewrite
  generation) and 0.50 s for Binoculars.

### Interpretability of `v` (App. F.7, the most relevant section for us)
- Projecting `v` onto the vocabulary (unembedding) gives junk: the maximum |cos| is 0.074, and the
  top tokens are multilingual fragments and subwords.
- The change in output logits caused by steering differs most between LLM and human text on tokens
  such as **"incumbent", "subsequently", "aforementioned", "plethora", "concurrently",
  "rationale"**. The authors note these are "associated with formal written expression", which
  points to a formality/register confound. The Spearman correlation between the vocabulary
  projection and the realized logit change is about 0.06.
- The effect on the final representation is **class-dependent**: the projection of
  `f_v - f` onto `mu_1 - mu_0` averages -1.67 for human text and +0.39 for LLM text. The same `v`
  therefore acts differently depending on context. It works through the downstream nonlinearity,
  rather than as a simple shift along a "sounds like AI" axis.

## Limitations
- The paper's own limitations: detection is document-level only (no localization in
  mixed-authorship text), a single vMF model may not fit across domains, and the steering is a
  single additive vector.
- **Additional limitations (our reading)**:
  1. **There is no generation-time steering or causal test of style.** `v` is optimized to be
     useful as a feature transform for a downstream classifier. It is not an "AI-style direction"
     in the model's own generation.
  2. **There are no confound controls.** Formality, domain, length, topic and register are not
     controlled. DetectRL human and LLM texts share prompts but differ in length and register. The
     logit analysis itself shows formal-register tokens.
  3. **Older generators** (GPT-3.5, PaLM, Claude-Instant, Llama-2) are used in the main benchmark.
     OpAI-Bench partly addresses this.
  4. The comparison with probes is confounded by the readout. Unsteered linear probes are nearly as
     good.
  5. Some detectors' TPR@0.01% values are interpolated.

# Skim: Stress-testing MGT Detection: Shifting LLM Writing Style to Fool Detectors
(Pedrotti, Papucci, et al., arXiv 2505.24523, 2025). Code: https://github.com/gpucce/control_mgt

- **Method**: build parallel (HWT, MGT) pairs by prompting an LLM with the title from XSum (BBC
  news) or arXiv abstracts. Then **DPO-fine-tune the generator with LoRA (r=32, attention layers)
  using HWT as "preferred" and its own MGT as "dispreferred"**. There are two variants:
  - `dpo`: random pairs.
  - `dpo-ling`: pairs chosen with the largest gap on the top-10 features of a linear SVM trained on
    ProfilingUD features (~130 morphosyntactic/syntactic features).
  - Training sets have about 6-7k pairs. The process can be iterated (dpo-2).
- **Generators**: Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct and Gemma-2-2B-it.
- **Detectors**: MAGE (Longformer), RADAR (adversarially trained RoBERTa-L), LLM-DetectAIve
  (DeBERTa), Binoculars (Falcon pair), and an in-domain SVM and RoBERTa.
- **Results (macro F1)**: one DPO round cuts detector performance by 5-35 points. On Llama/XSum,
  Binoculars falls from 0.99 to 0.33, MAGE from 0.76 to 0.40 and in-domain RoBERTa from 1.00 to
  0.52. TPR@1% FPR: MAGE 0.054 -> 0.014, DetectAIve 0.31 -> 0.001, RADAR 0.93 -> 0.62/0.32. RADAR
  holds up best. arXiv abstracts change less: in-domain SVM/RoBERTa stay at about 0.92-0.99.
  Random-pair DPO fools detectors more than linguistically selected pairs, because it shifts a wider
  range of features. dpo-ling aligns the targeted features more closely (MANOVA Pillai trace 0.76
  -> 0.71).
- **Features that shift toward human**: type/token ratio, clause length, numeral distribution
  (Llama), POS distribution (NOUN/ADJ/PRON/PUNCT), characters per token (Gemma), lexical density,
  and post-verbal subjects.
- **Human raters** (Prolific, 5 per pair) are near chance (40-60%, Fleiss kappa 0.06-0.10) both
  before and after DPO. DPO made Llama slightly *easier* for humans and Gemma harder.
- **Relevance**: this is the **weight-space (fine-tuning) analogue of what we want to do with
  activation steering**. It shows that the "detectable AI style" can be reduced cheaply, which is
  a causal manipulation of generation measured by independent detectors. It gives us a baseline and
  a set of detectors and metrics. It also warns that detector-fooling can happen through a broad
  feature shift rather than a single interpretable feature. A sensible comparison: does a single
  residual direction achieve a comparable F1 drop to one DPO round?

# Skim: Do LLMs write like humans? Variation in grammatical and rhetorical styles
(Reinhart, Markey, Laudenbach, Pantusen, Yurko, Weinberg, Brown; arXiv 2410.16107v2, PNAS draft 2025)

- **How the parallel corpus was built**: take human texts from many genres. Split each into two
  consecutive ~500-word chunks at sentence boundaries. Give **chunk 1** to an LLM with the
  instruction to "write ~500 more words in the same style, tone, and diction". Compare the output
  with the human **chunk 2**. This holds topic, genre and context fixed while varying authorship.
  - **HAP-E** (Human-AI Parallel English): 12k texts in six genres (academic, news, fiction, spoken
    word/podcast transcripts, blogs, TV/movie scripts). After dropping refusals and unusable
    outputs, 8,290 texts and 66,320 chunks remain.
  - **CAP** (COCA AI Parallel): built from COCA's eight registers. 9,615 texts and 76,920 chunks.
  - **Generators**: GPT-4o, GPT-4o-mini, and Llama-3 8B, 70B, 8B-Instruct and 70B-Instruct. The
    base and instruct pairs allow a clean comparison of instruction tuning.
  - Generalization check: M4 arXiv abstracts generated by GPT-3.5.
  - (The HAP-E corpus is reportedly on HuggingFace, possibly as `browndw/human-ai-parallel-corpus`.
    This name is from memory and not stated in the paper, so verify it before relying on it.)
- **Features**: Biber's 66 lexico-grammatical features via pseudobibeR. Differences are tested with
  the paired Wilcoxon test plus Bonferroni correction.
- **Results**:
  - A 7-way random forest gets 66% accuracy (chance 14%). Only 4.2% of LLM texts are called human
    and 9.8% of human texts are called LLM. Most errors are between sizes of the same family.
  - Pairwise human vs single-LLM classification gets 93-98% (RF), also when trained on HAP-E and
    tested on CAP. Lasso is similar, except for the **Llama base models at ~75%**.
  - **Instruction-tuned models are the outliers**:
    - Present participial clauses at 2-5x the human rate (GPT-4o 5.3x, d=1.38).
    - "That" clauses as subject at 2.6x.
    - **Nominalizations at 1.5-2x** (GPT-4o 2.1x, d=1.23).
    - Phrasal coordination at 1.9x.
    - Longer mean word length, more attributive adjectives.
    - Agentless passive at roughly 0.5x in GPT-4o.
    - Clausal coordination is avoided by GPT-4o but overused by Llama.
    - Downtoners: more in GPT-4o, fewer in Llama.
    - Overall a dense, noun-heavy, informational style **even when asked to continue informal
      speech**, which is genre misalignment.
  - **Vocabulary**: GPT-4o uses "camaraderie", "tapestry", "intricate", "palpable", "amidst",
    "solace", "fleeting", "unravel" and "underscore" at about 80-170x the human rate. "Tapestry"
    appears in 23% of GPT-4o outputs and "amidst" in 27%. Llama-Instruct overuses "unease",
    "palpable", "pang", "reminder", "prioritize" and "grapple". Obscenities are about 100x rarer.
  - **Base vs instruct**: the Llama-3 base models are close to human rates on most features, and
    the instruct versions are far off. The authors conclude that **instruction tuning, not
    pretraining data, introduces the AI style**. Larger models are not more human-like.
  - Generalization is weak. RFs trained on base-model outputs are at chance on M4/GPT-3.5. RFs
    trained on instruct outputs transfer above chance.
- **Artifacts to watch for**: instruct outputs begin with "Here is the continuation...". Base
  models repeat themselves (one document repeats "Deborah" 308 times).

### Confound list derived from 2410.16107
These are the things a "sounds like AI" direction might really be:
1. Nominalization and noun density, i.e. **informational density / formality / academic register**.
2. Participial clauses and phrasal coordination (syntactic complexity).
3. Mean word length and lexical sophistication (rare "fancy" words).
4. A specific lexicon (tapestry/palpable/camaraderie/delve). This would be a lexical direction, not
   a style direction.
5. Genre or register (spoken vs written). Human text spans many registers, while LLM text collapses
   toward one.
6. Sentiment and positivity (vibrant, solace) and summative "grandiose" sentences.
7. Profanity and informality (LLMs avoid them).
8. The instruction-tuning / assistant-persona signal itself. This is base vs instruct, the cleanest
   separable variable.
9. Formatting artifacts ("Here is the continuation...") and repetition in base models.

# Implications for our experiments

1. **S2D does not answer our question, but it supports part (a) strongly.** Human/LLM
   separability is linearly available in mid-to-late residual streams of 7-8B base models
   (Llama-3.1-8B, Qwen2.5-7B, Falcon-7B). Plain logistic regression on pooled late-layer states
   reaches about 0.96-0.99 AUROC. We should expect a diff-of-means or LR probe to work, and we
   should not claim separability as novel. **Our novelty is (b) causal generation steering and
   (c) disentanglement.**
2. **Layer choice**: S2D found intermediate layers (l/L ≈ 0.25-0.5; layer 11-12 of 32 in 8B Llama)
   to be the best intervention points, and late layers with last-token pooling to be the best
   readout. For generation steering, sweep injection layers over roughly 0.25-0.6 depth, and read
   out probes from several layers, pooled over the last 25% of tokens or with a mean.
3. **A single-vector read-time intervention acts in a class-dependent, nonlinear way**, and its
   vocabulary projection says little. Do not interpret our direction only through logit-lens or
   unembedding projection. Evaluate it behaviorally on generations.
4. **Formality confound, flagged by S2D's own analysis**: the tokens most affected by steering are
   formal register words. Together with Reinhart's finding (nominalizations, word length, dense
   style), **formality / informational density is the prime confound**. Build an explicit formality
   direction, for example from formal vs informal human text with matched content, and report:
   - the cosine between the AI and formality directions;
   - probe accuracy after projecting out formality;
   - whether steering with the residualized AI direction still moves detector scores.
5. **Use a Reinhart-style parallel design to control topic and genre.** Continuations of human
   prefixes (HAP-E style) hold content constant, so a diff-of-means direction is less contaminated
   by topic and domain. Compare base and instruct versions of the same model to isolate the
   assistant-persona and instruction-tuning component. For example:
   - direction from base-model vs instruct-model continuations;
   - direction from human vs instruct text;
   - check whether they coincide.
6. **Evaluation of (b)**: use independent detectors that the direction was not trained on. The
   Pedrotti paper's set is MAGE, RADAR, Binoculars and Fast-DetectGPT. Add a Biber/ProfilingUD
   feature profile, for example measuring nominalization and participle rates and the
   tapestry/palpable lexicon, to show *what* changed. Pair this with content-preservation measures
   (embedding similarity to the unsteered output, or an LLM judge) and coherence measures
   (perplexity under a separate model, or a judge). Report TPR at low FPR as well as AUROC.
7. **Baseline for (b)**: the Pedrotti DPO result (one DPO round drops Binoculars F1 from 0.99 to
   0.33 on news) is the fine-tuning upper bound to compare activation steering against. Prompting
   ("write like a human, casually") is the cheap baseline. LLM paraphrasing ("Polish") is known to
   remove most detector signal, so it is a strong comparison point.
8. **Length and domain controls**: detector scores rise with length (S2D AUROC goes from 0.91 at
   256 tokens to 0.99 at 1k). Fix generation length, or report detector scores binned by length,
   so that steering effects are not confounded with length changes.
9. **Observer choice matters a lot** (Gemma-2 and Mistral do badly in S2D). If we use Gemma or
   Mistral, first check unsteered separability. Llama-3.x-8B and Qwen2.5-7B are safe defaults.
