# Notes: "Linear Probing Provides Robust and Efficient Detection of Machine-Generated Text"
Quaremba, Yan, Black, Vrandecic, Simperl (KCL / Wikimedia). arXiv 2608.24780v1, 25 Aug 2026. 24 pages, all read (main + appendix A-D).

## 1. Research question
- Are machine-generated text (MGT) and human-written text (HWT) linearly separable in an LLM's latent space? Why? Can this be exploited for robust (OOD), sample-efficient detection?
- RQ1 ID performance; RQ2 OOD; RQ3 why probes transfer (shared direction); RQ4 sample efficiency; RQ5 does the direction encode a continuous degree of AI editing?
- Purely a **detection / representation-reading** paper. Detection is done on the *reader* model's activations on externally generated text (Llama-3-8B reads text produced by GPT-4, Cohere, Mistral, etc.).

## 2. Method (exact)
- **Reader LM**: Llama-3-8B (Grattafiori 2024; d=4096, 32 layers; plots show layers 0..32). Ablations: Llama-3B, Llama-1B, Qwen(3)-0.6B/4B/8B/32B.
- **Token pooling**: **last-token** hidden state at each layer (residual stream). Mean pooling ablated and is worse (see Sec. 7).
- **Dimensionality reduction**: per layer, PCA fit on training activations, keep top **k=100** PCs (100/4096 = 2.4%). PCA-vs-no-PCA nearly identical (no-PCA is actually slightly better by ~+0.01 AUC on TSM).
- **Probe**: sklearn LogisticRegression, L2 penalty, **C=1** (C in {0.01, 0.1, 10} makes ~no difference). No explicit mention of feature standardization beyond PCA (PCA centers the data).
- **Direction**: v^(l) = w^(l)/||w^(l)||_2 (normalized LR weight vector). Score = projection s^(l) = h^(l)ᵀ v^(l) (projection onto unit probe vector, *not* the LR probability; in the PCA space).
- **LLP (Layer-Averaged Linear Probe)**: one LR per layer, final score = mean over all L layers of projection scores (ensemble, following Nordby et al. 2026).
- **CLP (Concatenated-Layer Probe)**: one LR on concatenation of all layers' 100-d PCA features (L x 100 dims).
- AUC computed from the continuous scores. Each probe (incl. activation extraction and inference) takes 1-3 min on one A100-40GB.

## 3. Data, splits, sizes
- Representation analysis (Sec. 3): M4GT (Wang 2024), Wikipedia subset primarily; also Reddit, PeerRead, ArXiv; generators bloomz, cohere, dolly, gpt4.
- Detection: 4 benchmarks x 4 subsets = 16 settings; per subset **1,500 train / 500 test**, label-balanced, random sample.
  - DetectRL (Task 2 domains: ArXiv, Reddit, Yelp, News/XSum) - multiple generators + adversarial attacks mixed in.
  - MultiSocial (languages en, de, ru, zh; social media, short noisy text).
  - RAID (generators: Cohere, GPT4, Llama, Mistral; mix of domains/decoding/attacks).
  - TSM-Bench (Wikipedia English; tasks: FP first paragraph, PE paragraph extension (mixed human-machine), SUM summarization, TST text style transfer; six generators).
- AI-editing: APT-Eval (15k AI-polished texts; 200 each of 4 polish levels: extremely minor / minor / slightly major / major = 800), EditLens (reviews, creative writing, web articles, news; 3 LLM editors; same sampling). 3-way EditLens classification: 1,000/200/200 train/val/test.
- M4GT domain subsets saturated, which is why harder benchmarks were used.

## 4. Baselines (16)
- Zero-shot: Likelihood, LLR, Rank, GECScore, Revise, RAIDAR, FastDetectGPT, Binoculars (all with Llama-3-8B where applicable; rewriting via Llama-3-8B-Instruct).
- Supervised: EditLens (RoBERTa-large release), ID (PHD intrinsic dim + LR, XLM-R), OpenAI-RoBERTa (GPT-2 detector), RADAR (Vicuna-7B), RepreGuard (first PC of HWT-MGT activation differences, mean-pooled, full space ~ diff-in-means), BiScope, TextFluoroscopy (single middle layer, nonlinear MLP, Llama-3-8B), fine-tuned RoBERTa-base / XLM-R-base (bs 32, lr 2e-5, wd 0.01, 2 epochs).
## 5. Metrics
- AUC (ROC) main; ECE for calibration; accuracy/macro-F1 for 3-way; Pearson r for editing correlation.

## 6. Key numbers
### Linear separability (Sec 3.1; M4GT Wikipedia, Llama-8B)
- PC1/PC2 of last-token hidden states: overlapping at layer 0, **clearly linearly separable from layer 6** onward through layer 32 (same for Reddit, PeerRead, ArXiv; Fig 1, 7).
- MLP probes of depth 1-5 on mid-layer reps: AUC **decreases** with depth (e.g., ArXiv ID ~0.84 at depth 1 -> ~0.75 at depth 5; Wikipedia ~0.96 -> ~0.93), both ID and OOD -> boundary predominantly linear. (Note: depth-1 MLP on single middle layer ArXiv ~0.84 is much lower than LLP's 1.00 on DetectRL ArXiv; different datasets.)
- t-SNE: each domain/generator forms its own cluster that splits internally into human vs machine.
### Representation quality (Sec 3.2)
- MGT vs HWT across layers: MGT lower entropy (~1-1.5 vs ~3.5-4 nats on Wikipedia), lower effective rank (~60-90 vs 120-160), higher anisotropy (~0.15-0.17 vs ~0.08-0.12 mid layers), lower intrinsic dim (~13-20 vs ~25-35, TwoNN). Pattern holds across M4GT domains, DetectRL, RAID, MultiSocial, TSM though weaker under adversarial attacks/multiple generators.
### In-domain AUC (Table 1)
- LLP: DetectRL 1.000/1.000/1.000/1.000; MultiSocial en .957, de .934, ru .921, zh .944; RAID Cohere .971, GPT4 .993, Llama .995, Mistral .993; TSM FP .971, PE .942, SUM .978, TST .896.
- CLP nearly identical (e.g., TSM PE .920, TST .891).
- Best baselines: fine-tuned RoBERTa (e.g., TSM PE .754, TST .792), TextFluoroscopy, BiScope, Binoculars (strong zero-shot on DetectRL/RAID but ~0.5 on TSM). Gains +0.04 to +18.85 AUC (largest on TSM PE).
- Adversarial attacks (DetectRL; Table 3): LLP mixing .9993, paraphrase .9999, perturbation 1.000, prompt .9982 (saturated).
### OOD AUC (Table 2; mean of 3 transfers within benchmark)
- LLP: DetectRL ArXiv .983, Reddit .980, Yelp .990, News .944; MultiSocial en .932, de .882, ru .895, zh .870; RAID .927/.981/.991/.982; TSM FP .951, PE .809, SUM .951, TST .856. Gains up to +11.89 vs best baseline (RoBERTa). "+11 AUC OOD" headline.
- CLP is worse OOD than LLP (e.g., News .926, zh .804; fails ru->zh .71). Authors attribute CLP's News drop to overfitting stylistic cues of formal writing.
- Full matrix (Fig 10) e.g. LLP DetectRL xsum(News)-> Reddit .93, Reddit->News .89; TSM PE->TST .80 (LLP). Weakest transfers involve News, zh, PE, TST.
### Layer curves (App C.6, Fig 15)
- Single-layer LLP-style probe AUC: sharp rise to ~layer 5 (ID) / ~layer 10 (OOD), then plateau. OOD: middle layers best; slight degradation at final layers. First layer alone is terrible (-0.32 to -0.43 AUC vs LLP); last layer alone -0.04 to -0.08 vs LLP. Layer with highest OOD AUC used for vector analysis = **layer 16 of 32 (~50% depth)**.
### Shared MGT direction (Sec 5.4, App C.3)
- Cosine similarity of layer-16 LLP vectors (in PCA-100 space; note: each subset has its own PCA basis unless shared - paper is not explicit about how vectors from different PCA bases are compared; Fig 11 uses "shared PCA space") across all 16 subsets: high within benchmark (red blocks, roughly 0.5-0.9 by colormap), moderate across benchmarks (~0.1-0.5), some near zero. No exact numbers reported; only a heatmap.
- Fig 11: centroid-difference (diff-in-means) directions in shared layer-16 PCA space for DetectRL domain pairs are roughly parallel; Reddit most divergent. Authors acknowledge confounds (attacks, length, style).
### Sample efficiency (Sec 5.5, Fig 5, Fig 12)
- Near-peak with **3.3-6.7% of 1,500 = ~50-100 samples** (paper says 10-100). E.g., DetectRL ArXiv ~0.97 at 0.7% (~10 samples). Low seed variance vs RoBERTa/RepreGuard. Same without PCA (Fig 12). Caveat: in Fig 5 PCA was fit on full train set.
### Continuous "machineness" (Sec 5.6, App C.5, C.8)
- APT-Eval (800 samples): LLP projection score vs semantic similarity r=-0.586, Levenshtein r=0.728, Jaccard r=0.710. CLP: -0.442/0.584/0.588.
- EditLens: LLP -0.494/0.609/0.613; CLP -0.496/0.609/0.604.
- Probes trained only on binary labels (which training set is used for these projections is not clearly stated).
- 3-way (human / AI-edited / AI-generated) on EditLens with two thresholds on the 1-D score: LLP acc .848, macro-F1 .848; CLP .854/.853; RoBERTa .652/.642; EditLens Llama-3.2-3B .525/.438.
### Other
- Text length (DetectRL ArXiv, Table 4): LLP AUC .850 @25 chars, .908 @100, .966 @125, .981 @150, .993 @200, .997 @400. RoBERTa slightly better at <=50 chars.
- Calibration ECE (DetectRL): LLP .005-.010; CLP .000-.007; RoBERTa .002-.033.
- Ablations on TSM (Table 7 LLP; baseline .971/.942/.978/.896): mean pooling -0.019/-0.155/-0.021/-0.085; first layer only -0.39..-0.43; last layer only -0.036..-0.077; Llama-3B ~0; Llama-1B -0.01..-0.03; Qwen-8B +0.010..+0.031; Qwen-4B +0.007..+0.029; Qwen-32B +0.013..+0.048; Qwen-0.6B -0.005..-0.036; k=10 PCs -0.05..-0.12; k=50 -0.01..-0.02; k>=150 ~+0.005; no PCA +0.007..+0.016. CLP (Table 9) similar: mean pooling -0.008/-0.088/+0.001/-0.036.

## 7. Steering / causal interventions
- **None.** No activation addition, ablation, or generation experiments. Steering only mentioned in related work (Zou et al. RepE, Rimsky CAA). Limitations explicitly call for future work on "stability, causality, and universality of MGT directions" and say results "do not establish the existence of a universal latent MGT direction". So the causal question (our (b)) is entirely open from this paper.

## 8. Confound controls
- Length: only a train-length ablation (truncating to n chars) on DetectRL ArXiv; no length-matched human vs machine control, no length regression.
- Domain: handled via OOD cross-domain/language/generator/task transfer and t-SNE showing domain clusters; no explicit domain-direction orthogonalization.
- Formality / style: **not controlled**. They themselves blame CLP's News failure on "stylistic cues associated with formal writing" and note centroid directions differ due to "stylistic or grammatical variation" - i.e., style/formality is an acknowledged but unquantified confound.
- Assistant persona: not considered. Reader model is base Llama-3-8B (not instruct), so chat-template/persona effects are not studied.
- Topic/content matching: TSM and APT-Eval/EditLens have partially paired content (same Wikipedia topic / edited human text), the others are not content-matched.

## 9. Limitations (authors + mine)
- Authors: 4 rep-quality metrics non-exhaustive; no systematic scale study; only logistic regression; universality/causality not established; AI-edit analysis preliminary; needs white-box models.
- Mine: single reader model main results; DetectRL/M4GT saturated (AUC 1.0), so headline gains come from TSM/MultiSocial; probability that the direction encodes "low-perplexity/compressed text" (entropy/ID findings) rather than AI style per se; no human-vs-AI-text-of-same-content control except TSM; cosine heatmap numbers not reported and PCA-basis comparison unclear; no statistical tests beyond seed shading.

- Recipe: forward text through base LM with output_hidden_states; take h[:, -1, :] for every layer; per layer StandardScaler-free PCA(100) fit on train; LogisticRegression(penalty='l2', C=1); v = coef/||coef||; score = PCA(h)·v; LLP = mean over layers; AUC via roc_auc_score. Intrinsic dim via scikit-dimension TwoNN.
- Note: to use v as a steering vector in the residual stream, map back from PCA space: v_full = PCA.components_.T @ v (and remember PCA centering - only the direction matters for steering).

## Implications for our experiments
1. **Reading (a) is easy - expect near-ceiling AUC.** Linear probes on last-token residual activations give AUC ~0.93-1.00 ID with ~100 examples. A high AUC alone is not evidence of a meaningful "sounds like AI" direction; we must report controls (length-matched, topic-matched, formality-matched pairs) and OOD transfer. Use paired/topic-matched data (e.g., human text vs AI rewrite of same content, like TSM/APT-Eval) to suppress domain confounds.
2. **Layer choice**: signal appears from ~15% depth (layer 5-6 of 32), plateaus; best OOD around **~30-60% depth (layer 10-20 of 32; they used layer 16 = 50%)**; final layers degrade OOD. For steering, target middle layers (~40-60% depth); sweep 25-75%.
3. **Pooling**: last-token beat mean pooling (up to -0.155 AUC with mean pooling on paragraph-extension). For reading, use last token (or compare both). For steering, though, a direction learned from last-token states may encode "summary/end-of-text" features; consider also mean-pooled diff-in-means directions and test which steers better. Report both.
4. **Direction estimation**: LR (L2, C=1) on PCA-100 features is robust and insensitive to C; diff-in-means (RepreGuard-like) was weaker for detection, but diff-in-means is often the better *steering* vector. Compute both and compare cosine. Map PCA-space weights back to residual space before steering.
5. **Multi-layer ensemble** (LLP) beats any single layer for detection; for steering, multi-layer injection with per-layer vectors is an option, but start with single mid layer.
6. **Causality is untested** - our (b) is a genuine contribution. Measure with an independent detector (e.g., Binoculars, fine-tuned RoBERTa, or EditLens/Pangram-style model) plus coherence (perplexity under another model) and content preservation (semantic similarity to unsteered output).
7. **Confounds to explicitly test (c)**: the paper's own evidence hints at formality/style leakage (CLP News failure) and that MGT = "compressed, low-entropy, anisotropic" representations - the direction may be partly a perplexity/predictability or formality axis. Build formality, length, domain, and assistant-persona directions and report cosine similarities + detection AUC after projecting them out.
8. **Continuous-ness**: projection scores correlate r~0.6-0.73 with edit strength - suggests a graded axis, good sign for dose-response steering. Use APT-Eval/EditLens-like graded data to validate that our direction is monotonic.
9. **Model**: Qwen (4B/8B) gave slightly better separability than Llama-3-8B; even 1B-3B models lose little. Smaller models (Qwen3-4B, Llama-3.2-3B) are viable for our compute budget.
10. **Short texts**: AUC drops to ~0.85 below ~100 chars; use >=150-char texts (ideally paragraphs) for both probing and evaluating steered generations.
11. **Pitfall**: fit PCA/probes on train only; balance labels; avoid generator/domain leakage between train and test; report OOD splits (cross-domain, cross-generator) since in-domain is saturated.
