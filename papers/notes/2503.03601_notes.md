# Notes: Feature-Level Insights into Artificial Text Detection with Sparse Autoencoders
Kuznetsov, Kushnareva, Druzhinina, Razzhigaev, Voznyuk, Piontkovskaya, Burnaev, Barannikov (Skoltech / AIRI / MIPT), arXiv:2503.03601v1, Mar 2025. Short paper (4 pp main text + 18 pp appendix). All 22 pages read (8 chunks).

## 1. Method
- **Model**: Gemma-2-2B (the paper does not say base vs. -it; context implies base). Residual-stream SAEs from **Gemma Scope** (Lieberum et al. 2024).
- **Which SAEs / widths**: the paper never states the width or L0 variant. Feature IDs go up to ~16302, so these are almost certainly the **16k-width (16384) residual SAEs**, L0 variant not given. One SAE per layer; **every even layer 0-24** was used for classification.
- **Pooling**: text-level vector = **sum over all tokens** of the SAE feature activations at layer l, i.e. f = sum_i f^(l)(x_i^(l)). (The baseline "Embeddings" uses **mean-pooled** raw residual activations.) Note that sum pooling makes features length-dependent, and they test length sensitivity separately (App. D).
- **Classifiers**: (1) **XGBoost** on the full per-layer SAE feature vector, used both to measure expressiveness and to rank feature importance. (2) **Single-feature threshold classifiers**, scored by macro-F1 per domain and per generator subset.
- **Interpretation**, done three ways: (a) manual reading of max-activating texts; (b) **steering + GPT-4o** auto-interpretation of how generations change; (c) comparison with Neuronpedia labels. For manual interpretation they took layers 8-20, the XGBoost top-20 features, plus the best threshold feature for each domain and each model.
- Feature browser: https://mgtsaevis.github.io/mgt-sae-visualization/

## 2. Datasets
- **COLING 2025 GenAI Detection Task 1** (English, binary human vs. machine; Wang et al. 2025). They train on Train only and evaluate on Dev (similar distribution), Devtest and Test (distinct distributions). Generators include LLaMA 7-65B, LLaMA-3 8/70B, GLM-130B, Bloom/Bloomz, Cohere, davinci-001/002/003, gpt-3.5-turbo, GPT-4, GPT-4o, T5/flan-T5/T0, Gemma-7b-it, Gemma2-9b-it, GPT-J, GPT-NeoX, Mixtral-8x7B, OPT 125M-30B, dolly. Domains: arxiv, cmv, eli5, finance, hswag, medicine, open_qa, outfox, peerread, reddit, reddit_eli5, roct, sci_gen, squad, tldr, wiki_csai, wikihow, wikipedia, wp, xsum, yelp.
- **Dataset artefacts they found (App. B)** are important as confounds:
  - Human COLING texts often have a **space before commas** and **commas after line breaks** (preprocessing leftovers).
  - GPT-NeoX texts show the "...." long-ellipsis anomaly (~24% of samples).
  - **GPT-4o uses `\n\n` in ~98% of texts**, Mixtral ~80%, Gemma2-9b-it ~77%, LLaMA-3 ~67%, while humans use it in ~15%.
  - Lengths vary widely: T5/T0 outputs are ~50-100 tokens, GPT-4o ~570, human ~345 (Gemma tokens).
- **RAID** (Dugan et al. 2024): only ChatGPT, GPT-4 and human texts, 100 continuations per (domain, model, decoding, penalty, attack) combination. Used to find attack-sensitive features. Attacks covered: article deletion, homoglyph, whitespace, zero-width space, upper/lower case, synonym, numbers, paragraph insertion, alternative spelling, misspelling, DIPPER paraphrase.

## 3. Key results by layer (Fig. 2, macro-F1, values read off plots)
- Train/Dev: SAE ~0.92 at layer 0, rising to ~0.96 train / ~0.91-0.92 dev at layers 10-16. Embeddings are ~0.01-0.03 lower throughout.
- Devtest: SAE ~0.83 at L0, ~0.92 at L10-14, ~0.90 at L16-24. Embeddings are ~0.74-0.84, so SAE leads by about 0.07-0.1.
- Test: SAE ~0.66 at L0, peaking at **~0.84 at L16**, which beats the SOTA MTL system (Advacheck, ~0.83). Embeddings peak ~0.80.
- Conclusion: **mid layers (12-16) are best; layer 16 has the best generalization**, so all later feature analysis uses L16. Sparse features outperform mean-pooled dense activations ("removing superposition helps").

### GPT / model-specific
- **8689**: GPT-style verbose instructions. Highly sensitive to the "...." anomaly and to **sentence length**. Steering interpretation is **excessive synonym substitution** (negative steering makes the text repeat words). It is the top feature for GPT-3.5+ (F1 0.99-1.00 on gpt-35/gpt4/reddit).
- **14919**: detects the Bloom family. Sensitive to length and to the "...." and "\n ," anomalies.
- Fig. 16 model-specific examples: 11572, 13889 (dolly-v2 0.96), 9966, 6689 (gpt-35 0.98), 3644, 9525 (gpt4o 0.87).
- Other top features by model (Fig. 15): 11481, 10480, 6005, 10529, 4880, 15624, 15978.

### Domain-specific
- **12390** (arxiv, F1 1.00): linking words in dependent clauses. Steering makes syntax more or less complex.
- **6513** (finance): manually fires on informal, opinionated speech ("I mean", "like"). Steering interpretation is factual density vs. elaboration. In actual generations, +4 adds enthusiasm and opinion ("What a cool guy!") and −4 gives terse facts.
- **14953** (medicine): second-person directive recommendations ("You should..."). GPT-4o read it as **informal-to-formal tone**. In practice, +4 "turns every response into legal advice" ("contact your local office"), which degrades coherence.
- **4560** (reddit / reddit_eli5 0.95): informal internet discussion, first-person conjectures, "Yeah"/"Ah", timestamps. Steering gives speculative causal links.
- **4773** (wikipedia 0.82): repeated words in variant forms and corrupted words. Steering interpretation is hallucination; the authors also say it raises sophistication.
- **1416** (wikihow 0.93): parsing errors (missing formulas) and abnormal punctuation. Steering gives philosophical/metaphorical expansion, creativity in fiction and hallucination on factual prompts.
- 6953 (open_qa) appears in Fig. 4. Other domain top features (Fig. 14): 5014, 3184, 3847, 4432, 5159, 15856, 2177.

### Confound / superficial features (App. D, Tables 1-2)
Method: compare the top-10% longest vs. shortest human texts per domain, take the top-10 features by mean difference, and intersect across domains. Anomalies were handled the same way within the top-3 domains.
- **Length**: L16 1033 and 16028; L18 7373; L20 8684.
- Space-before-comma: L18 2199; L20 6631.
- "....": L16 2889, 8689, 14919; L18 3851, 12685, 16302; L20 8573, 11612, 12748.
- "\n ,": L16 14919, 16028; L18 12685; L20 8573, 12267.
- Markdown headers were found by hand (L16 1033, 15152), but these were not stable across domains. No stable `\n\n` feature was found.
- Attack-sensitive features (from the XGBoost top-10% only), L16: article deletion 3518, 13998; homoglyph 9266; whitespace 9266, 5627, 10229, 750; zero-width 9266, 10262; case 13998; synonym 4052, 9100, 13998. Layers 18 and 20 are listed in Table 2. These do **not** overlap with the best ATD features. No consistent features were found for numbers, paragraphs, spelling, misspelling or paraphrase.

## 5. Steering approach (exact)
- **Formula**: x' = x + λ · A_max · d_i. Here d_i is the SAE decoder column (W_dec) for feature i, A_max is the feature's maximum activation over a reference dataset (not specified, presumably COLING), and λ is a scale.
- **λ grid**: {−4, −3, −2.5, −2, −1.5, −1, −0.5, 0.5, 1, 1.5, 2, 2.5, 3, 4}.
- The shift is added at **all token positions** during generation; the authors note real texts activate the feature on only a few tokens. The layer is implicitly the SAE's layer (16). The paper does not state which model generates (presumably Gemma-2-2B), the decoding settings, or the number of prompts.
- **Evaluation is purely qualitative**: GPT-4o gets 50 original/modified generation sequences per feature (prompt in Fig. 17) and outputs a "possible function / effect type / observed behavior" table. Table 7 shows ±4 generations for 6513, 4773, 1416 and 14953 on three prompts (Moon, Atlantic Ocean, a Reddit eye-colour post).
- **What they observe**: features act as interpretable "dials", with labels reported as negative / neutral (0.5-1.5) / strong (≥2.0). At ±4, generations often lose factual accuracy or coherence (e.g., "first person to die on the Moon was a Catholic priest", "Atlantic separates Africa and Europe", the 14953 "contact your local office" loops).
- **No detector-based measurement of steering**: nobody checks whether steering makes text more or less detectable. There is also no measurement of content preservation or perplexity.

## 6. Domain / model statistics, takeaways
- Modern LLMs (GPT-3.5+, LLaMA, Gemma) are easily detected by general "overcomplexity/verbosity" features, especially in information-dense domains (finance, medicine, wiki_csai, reddit_eli5).
- arXiv is harder, which suggests GPT mimics scientific prose.
- The **hardest domains are outfox (student essays) and yelp (reviews)**: personalized, informal prompts make LLMs human-like and the general features fail (3608 F1 0.49 on outfox).
- Old or weak models (flan-T5, T0) score below chance on the universal features. OPT is the most "universal"-feature-detectable of the older families.
- Model classes appear distinct: weak (flan, t0); mid (opt, bloom, gpt_j, gpt_neo); modern (GPT-3.5+, LLaMA, Gemma).

## 7. Limitations (stated and observed)
- Stated: one SAE family on one model; new LLMs and attacks are not covered; some features are not interpretable.
- Observed:
  - SAE width/L0 and steering details are unspecified.
  - Steering evaluation is qualitative only (GPT-4o descriptions, no metrics, no detector, no coherence score).
  - Sum pooling is length-confounded.
  - COLING contains strong preprocessing artefacts (space-before-comma in human text, `\n\n` in chat models), and the classifier may exploit these.
  - The feature analysis covers layer 16 only.
  - Single-feature "general" features are confounded with formality, verbosity and domain by construction, and the authors make no attempt to disentangle them.
- **Code**: there is no code repository in the paper, only the feature visualization site https://mgtsaevis.github.io/mgt-sae-visualization/

## Implications for our experiments
1. **Model/SAE choice**: Gemma-2-2B + Gemma Scope residual 16k SAEs at **layer ~12-16** is a validated setup for human-vs-AI separation. Use mid layers for our probe/diff-of-means direction, with layer 16 as the default and a sweep over 8-20.
2. **Baseline steering method**: steer with the decoder directions of **L16 features 8264 (repetition, F1 ~1.0 on GPT-4/4o), 8689 (synonym substitution), 3608 (sentence complexity) and 6587 (long-winded intros)** using x' = x + λ·A_max·d_i, with λ in [−4, 4] at all positions. Compare against our "AI direction" on (a) independent detector score shift (e.g., Binoculars/RADAR/a RoBERTa detector), (b) content preservation, and (c) perplexity/coherence. This adds the quantitative evaluation the paper lacks. Expect coherence to break at |λ| ≥ 3.
3. **Confound directions to project out or compare cosine similarity with**:
   - formality: 14161, plus 14953 (casual-to-formal, second-person directives)
   - informal/opinionated register: 6513, 4560 (Reddit-ness)
   - length: L16 1033, 16028; L18 7373; L20 8684
   - punctuation/format artefacts: 2889, 14919, 16028 at L16; markdown 1033, 15152
   - domain: 12390 (arxiv), 1416 (wikihow), 4773 (wikipedia)

   Report cos(our direction, d_i) for each and re-test separation and steering after orthogonalizing against them.
4. **Data hygiene**: if we use COLING/RAID-style data, normalize whitespace (space-before-comma, `\n\n`, "....") and length-match the human and AI texts. Otherwise the direction may encode preprocessing artefacts or the chat-model `\n\n` habit rather than "AI-ness".
5. **Expected hard cases**: essays and reviews (outfox, yelp), and prompts asking for informal or personal style. These are good held-out tests of whether our direction is more than "verbosity/overcomplexity". A direction that still separates there would be evidence it is distinct from those features.
6. **Pooling**: report both mean and sum pooling, since sum pooling leaks length. The paper shows SAE features beat mean-pooled activations for classification, so an SAE-feature logistic probe is a reasonable baseline next to our dense probe.
7. **Persona confound**: the paper does not address the assistant persona. Features 8264 and 8689 ("GPT-style instructions") may partly be persona or instruction-following features. Test them on base vs. -it Gemma generations.
